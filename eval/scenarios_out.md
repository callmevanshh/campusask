## overload

**Q:** my cgpa is 6.5, can I take 24 credits in one semester?

**A:** According to the UG Regulations (Oct 2025), the rules regarding academic load and overloads are as follows:

* Normal load is 16-20 credits for first- and second-year students, and 16-22 credits for third- and fourth-year students [13].
* First-year students cannot take any overload [1].
* Second-year students require a CGPA of more than 8.0 to take an overload of up to 2 additional credits over 20 credits (making it up to 22 credits) [1].
* Third- and fourth-year students require a CGPA of more than 8.0 to take an overload of up to 2 additional credits over 22 credits (making it up to 24 credits) [1].
* Students with a CGPA of 8.0 or lower may be allowed to take an overload only in one semester in the final year (either 7th or 8th semester) [1].

Applying these rules to your numbers:
* Your CGPA is 6.5, which is not more than 8.0 [1]. 
* Taking 24 credits in a semester constitutes an overload of 4 credits over the normal load of 20 credits (for first/second years) or 2 credits over the normal load of 22 credits (for third/fourth years) [1, 13].
* Since your CGPA is 6.5, you are not eligible for this overload unless you are in your final year (7th or 8th semester) and receive permission [1].

Please confirm with the academic office or your degree audit on the official portal.

## honors

**Q:** i have cgpa 7.5, will i get honors?

**A:** According to the UG Regulations (Oct 2025), to graduate with Honors, a student must meet the following conditions:

* The student earns at least 12 extra discipline credits from in-class courses [7].
* The student’s program includes a B.Tech. project [11].
* The student has a CGPA of 8.0 or more at graduation [11].

With a CGPA of 7.5, you do not meet the requirement of having a CGPA of 8.0 or more at graduation [11].

**Problems:** none


**Q:** what is the minimum cgpa for an honors degree

**A:** According to the UG Regulations (Oct 2025), a student graduating with Honors must have a CGPA of **8.0** or more at graduation [8].
